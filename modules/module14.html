03 / Anchor the claimA figure should survive without its speaker.

We reuse the unchanged 365 daily observations of completed system-wide rentals in 2011. These are counts of completed rentals, not unique bikes, latent demand, station inventory, or unserved customers. Define the high state by $S_t=\mathbf1\{C_t\geq4000\}$; the threshold is a scenario definition, not measured capacity.

Observed daily completed rentals; dashed horizontal line: the assumed 4,000-rental state threshold. January–August supplies 243 days and 242 training pairs. September–December supplies 122 rolling one-day targets. These later dates have already been used in development.

Freeze the fitted matrix after August 31. For each later day, issue the chain probability using the previous observed state. Persistence predicts that previous state with probability one. Both methods receive the same information and are scored on the same dates:

$$q_t=\widehat P_{S_{t-1},1},\qquad q_t^{\mathrm{persist}}=S_{t-1},\qquad L=\frac1{122}\sum_{t\in\mathcal A}(q_t-S_t)^2.$$

Here $\mathcal A$ is the September–December target set. The first target is September 1 and its origin is August 31. These are rolling one-day probabilities with an updated observed origin, not a single 122-day path forecast. The model fit remains frozen.

A hand check before any data code

For $P=\begin{pmatrix}.8&.2\\.3&.7\end{pmatrix}$ and a low initial state, the high-state probability after two steps is $.8\times.2+.2\times.7=.30$. If the terminal observed state is 1, forecasts .30 and 0 incur losses $(.30-1)^2=.49$ and $(0-1)^2=1$. The probability is .30; its realized loss is .49. They answer different questions.

For the bike comparison, define an absolute reduction and a relative reduction using unrounded mean losses:

$$\Delta=L_{\mathrm{persist}}-L_{\mathrm{chain}},\qquad R=100\frac{\Delta}{L_{\mathrm{persist}}}\%\quad\text{when }L_{\mathrm{persist}}>0.$$

The absolute reduction is about 0.04642 in Brier-loss units; the relative reduction is about 20.98%. Say “approximately 21% lower mean Brier loss.” Do not rename it “21% more accurate” or a 21-percentage-point change in event probability. If the baseline loss is zero, relative reduction is undefined; report the two losses and absolute difference.

chain_loss = np.mean((forecast - observed_state)**2)
baseline_loss = np.mean((previous_state - observed_state)**2)
absolute_reduction = baseline_loss - chain_loss
relative_percent = (100 * absolute_reduction / baseline_loss
                    if baseline_loss > 0 else None)

Scoring context: Gneiting and Raftery, Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation (author-hosted PDF). We use the single-event binary Brier loss, with smaller values better, and compare matching forecast situations. The relative reduction is a descriptive summary, not an inferential test.

05 / Explain what changedKeep scenario effects and uncertainty separate.

Reuse the earlier conditional scenario: multiply $a=\widehat P_{01}$ by c, hold $b=\widehat P_{10}$ fixed, and adjust the first row to sum to one. For a low initial state and seven transitions, compute

$$P_c=\begin{pmatrix}1-ca&ca\\b&1-b\end{pmatrix},\qquad p_c=[(1,0)P_c^7]_1,\qquad \text{change in percentage points}=100(p_c-p_1).$$

Require $0\leq ca\leq1$; reject invalid scenarios rather than clipping them. A higher probability of a high completed-rental state is not automatically a desirable outcome and does not reveal a causal policy effect.

Describe the comparison in the right units

Exact matrix-power probabilities. No sampling bands or new simulated records.

Calculated probabilities under three assumed transition matrices, all starting low; the high-to-low entry is fixed. The dark outline highlights the selected scenario. Bars have no uncertainty interval because these are exact conditional calculations.

To explain simulation accuracy from Module 4, suppose N independent paths were simulated from a fixed matrix and $\hat p_N$ were their terminal high-state fraction. Its conditional standard error has an exact-model reference:

$$\operatorname{SE}(\hat p_N\mid P_c)=\sqrt{\frac{p_c(1-p_c)}N}\leq\frac1{2\sqrt N}.$$

Quadrupling N halves this standard error. It does not halve uncertainty about fitted parameters, the threshold, missing measurements, temporal stability, or whether a Markov model is adequate. At N=10,000 the bound is .005, or 0.5 percentage points; whether that is small enough depends on the decision. Here the probability is already computed exactly, so simulation is unnecessary.

06 / Individual explanationOwn the reasoning, including its limits.

Each student should be able to explain the complete project workflow, including code written with a partner or with consequential assistance. A useful answer starts with the target and units, defines the formula's symbols, traces a number, names a check, and predicts what changing an assumption would do.

Choose an explanation prompt

Rehearse aloud first. Then use the notebook to check the calculation and refine the wording.

Listen for evidence of understanding
PromptA useful response includes
Why this method?The question it answers, its assumptions, and a credible baseline
What does this symbol mean?A quantity, its units or dimensionless scale, and the conditioning information
How do you know this code is right?A hand case, independent formula, invariant, or deliberate failure test
What if an assumption changes?A prediction before computation, a controlled change, and a limit on interpretation
What can you not conclude?A concrete missing measurement, untested assumption, or unsupported causal claim

It is acceptable to say what additional evidence you would need. Saying a result is conditional is useful only if you can name the condition and explain why it matters.

07 / Final investigationsMake the presentation match the project.

Use these four investigations to refine the existing deliverables. They are unanswered in the companion notebook and create no additional assignment, concept lab, or project.

  1. E1 — Build a six-minute evidence narrative. For the existing project, outline six minutes of speaking time across question and units, data and assumptions, model and check, evidence and baseline, scenarios and uncertainty, and recommendation and limits. Build a claim–evidence ledger for three central claims, labeling evidence as observed, calculated, simulated, or assumed. Keep the project's two or three meaningful methods visible; no new project or extra submission is assigned.
  2. E2 — Make the central figure defensible. Reproduce one central project figure from a fresh kernel and write a stand-alone caption naming the target, units, data roles, baseline, and a decision-relevant limitation. Hand-check an absolute and a relative loss comparison, including the zero-baseline boundary. Explain why a percentage reduction in mean Brier loss is not a percentage increase in accuracy or a percentage-point change in event probability.
  3. E3 — Explain and modify your own calculation. Prepare an individual explanation of one formula and the code that implements it: define the symbols, target, units, and available information; trace one numerical calculation; name an independent check; then change one assumption and predict the direction or limits of the result before rerunning. Distinguish a conditional scenario comparison from an observed or causal effect, and computational uncertainty from model uncertainty.
  4. E4 — Audit the claim and finish the existing package. Audit the provided instructor-written AI-style overclaim without requiring an AI account. For each consequential claim, record independent evidence, a correction, and a remaining limitation. Check consistency across the existing executed notebook, 4–5-page report, one-page decision brief, and slides; document contributions and consequential assistance, and rehearse the six-minute talk and individual explanation. This is a final review of existing deliverables, not an additional assignment.

Instructor-written AI-style overclaim for E4

“Our Markov model is 21% more accurate, so increasing low-to-high transitions by 50% will prevent bike shortages. With 10,000 simulations, all uncertainty is negligible; the reused later period proves future performance.”

Audit each clause against an executed result or independent check. No AI account is required. If you use AI in your own project, keep a concise record of consequential suggestions, the checks applied, corrections made, and responsibility for the final interpretation. An AI's agreement is not independent evidence.
